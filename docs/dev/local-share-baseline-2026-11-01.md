## 1. Why this exists

The spike headline says one serialised prefill gate "caps local share at
~5.8 %". The same measured basis also divides to ~1.6 %. Both figures are
reproducible; they are **not the same quantity**. A downstream option
comparison that swaps one for the other (e.g. cost per locally-served token
against a lever's effect on "share") is wrong by a factor of 3.7.

This document pins the arithmetic: the window, the two denominators, the
ceiling under each, the ≥10 % requirement under each, and what could not be
reproduced.

## 3. The two denominators (AC1)

| Definition | Actual | Ceiling | Ceiling assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prompt-token share** — local prompt tokens / offered prompt tokens | **0.699 %** | **1.621 %** | none beyond the measured rate |
| **Stream share** — locally served streams / offered streams, capacity ÷ *current local* mean prompt size per stream | **2.573 %** (46/1 788) | **5.964 %** | every offered stream is locally servable **and** newly-served streams keep the current local prompt size |

The bridge between them is the prompt-size mix:

| Per-stream size | Tokens | Streams the ceiling buys |
|---|---|---|
| current **local** streams | 19 085 (877 908 / 46) | 106.6 → **5.96 %** |
| all offered streams | 70 234 (125.6 M / 1 788) | 29.0 → **1.62 %** |
| **remote** streams | 71 585 (124.7 M / 1 742) | 28.4 → **1.59 %** |
| refused admission-guard prompts (20k–32k band midpoint) | 26 000 | 78.3 → **4.38 %** |

A remote stream carries **3.75×** the prompt tokens of a local stream
(71 585 vs 19 085), and the offered streams average 70 234 tokens — so the
stream ceiling is **3.68×** the token ceiling (exactly
`offered mean / local mean` = 70 234 / 19 085). The published ~5.8 % is this
table's first row quoted to 0.1 pp; the recomputation gives **5.96 %** against
the published 2 820 s basis (Δ 0.13 pp, from rounding 2.03 M / 2.82 ks / 2.6 %
— see §7).

## 4. Reconciliation verdict (AC1)

- **~1.6 % is the ceiling on local share of offered *prompt tokens*** — the
  denominator the 44 300 tok/s and 718 tok/s figures already use. It needs no
  assumption beyond the measured rate.
- **~5.8 % (recomputed 5.96 %) is the ceiling on local share of *streams***,
  under the assumption that expansion preserves the current local prompt-size
  mix. The assumption is what makes it 3.7× larger, and it is **not**
  reachable by expanding into the refused population: the streams that would
  be newly served are precisely the large ones being refused
  (`large_context_bypass` 32.8 %, `context_too_large` 16.6 %), which carry
  more prompt tokens each. At the refused-band midpoint the ceiling is
  **4.38 %**; at the offered-stream mean it collapses to **1.62 %** — the same
  number as the token ceiling.
- **Neither is unconditionally "correct"**; the definition must be stated.
  Practical rule for children 2–6:
  - the parent AC9 target is a **stream share** → quote the stream ceiling
    (5.96 %, mix-assumption labelled) and note the 1.6–4.4 % mix-corrected
    range;
  - anything costed **per token** (throughput levers, compaction, quantisation
    or slot geometry — a tok/s gain) → quote the **token** ceiling (1.62 %),
    because a tok/s lever multiplies tokens, not stream counts;
  - never mix the two in one comparison.

### Why the other figure was produced — candidate explanations tested

| Candidate cause | Test | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **streams vs prompt tokens** | The 5.8 %-implied denominator is **34.9 M tokens**; the stream-equivalent denominator (1 788 streams × 19 085 local tokens) is **34.1 M** — the same quantity within 2.8 %. Ratio between the ceilings equals the offered/local mean prompt sizes (70 234 / 19 085 = 3.68). | **Confirmed cause.** |
| peak vs average offered load | Neither figure uses a peak: both divide by the window mean (44 295 tok/s offered; 1 223 s busy of 2 835 s available). The ceiling model is already 100 %-duty-cycle, so a peak-load denominator would *lower* the ceiling rather than raise it. | **Ruled out.** |
| cached vs uncached tokens | An uncached-token denominator would have to be 34.9 M = 27.8 % of the offered 125.6 M, i.e. an average `cached_ratio` of only 0.72. The project-level measured median is 0.952 (792 dispatches, 2026-10-18 — indicative, not this window), which would put an uncached denominator near 6 M and the ceiling near 34 %. | **Ruled out.** |

## 5. The ≥10 % requirement

| Target definition | Local prompt tokens needed | Multiple of single-gate capacity |
|---|---|---|
| 10 % of offered prompt tokens | **12,557,778** | **6.17×** |
| 10 % of offered streams at the current local mix | **3,412,390** | **1.68×** |
| Published "~4.2 M = 10 % of ~42 M" | 4 200 000 | 2.06× |

The published 4.2 M is **not reproducible from this window**: no denominator
in it is ~42 M (offered prompt tokens are 125.6 M; the stream-equivalent
denominator is 34.1 M, and 10 % of the offered prompt tokens is 12.56 M).
4.2 M is 2.06× the single-gate capacity, so the accompanying "more than 2×
capacity" claim does hold — it is the "10 % of ~42 M" justification that does
not.

## 7. Caveats and tolerances

- **2 820 s vs 2 835 s.** The published extrapolation used 47.0 min; the
  window is 47.25 min. Effect: ceiling 2 024 285 vs 2 035 057 tokens
  (−0.53 %), immaterial to every conclusion. The tool reports both.
- **The offered-load denominator is a lower bound.** Of 1 781
  `Stream finished` lines, 177 carry `tokens=missing` (OpenCode 170, MunitoAI
  6, local 1) and contribute nothing to the 125.6 M. Including them would
  raise the denominator, lowering both the actual and the ceiling token
  shares. Every share here is therefore an **upper bound**.
- **718 tok/s is a blended delivered-token rate**, not a raw prefill rate: it
  is delivered prompt tokens (including cached-prefix re-sends) per
  busy-second (busy itself an upper bound, dispatch → finish). The engine's
  own `prompt eval` lines report a median 176.1 tok/s (p90 574.3, n = 864).
  Extrapolating the blended rate to a *cold, large-prompt* population — which
  is what expansion into the refused traffic means — is optimistic: the
  ceiling is an order-of-magnitude statement, not a forecast.
- **Concurrency headroom is not in the model.** Local busy averaged 1.06
  concurrent streams with a peak of 2, so the ceiling extrapolation already
  assumes the gate is busy for the whole window; the gain being computed is
  from *idle* gate time, not from parallel prefill.
- **Fallback-event vs decision counters** are not interchangeable (§6).
